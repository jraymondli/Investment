SGOV is extremely low-risk compared with stocks or longer-term bond funds, although it isn't FDIC-insured and its share price can fluctuate slightly.

## Why I like SGOV for parking money

The biggest advantage for you as a California resident is its favorable state-tax treatment.

For 2025, approximately 95.14% of SGOV's dividend income was attributable to U.S. government obligations, generally eligible for exemption from California income tax. The percentage must be checked each year.&#x20;

| High-yield savings         | High, within FDIC limits | Bank withdrawals                    | Fully taxable         |

The distinction is that SGOV has a tiny amount of market-price risk, while a Treasury bill held to maturity returns its face value, assuming the U.S. government pays as promised.

## Potential drawbacks

- Interest-rate changes: If the Federal Reserve reduces short-term rates, SGOV's yield will gradually decline.
- Not FDIC-insured: It is a securities investment, not a bank deposit.
- Market hours and settlement: You sell it like a stock; trades normally settle in one business day.
- Tax reporting: You generally receive a 1099-DIV rather than a 1099-INT, and may need to make the California adjustment using BlackRock's annual tax supplement.

## My recommendation

I would be comfortable using SGOV for cash you expect to need in 1–12 months, as long as you can tolerate small price fluctuations and don't need instant access outside trading hours.

For cash you know you won't need for three or six months, I'd also compare direct Treasury bills. They lock in their yield until maturity, whereas SGOV continually reinvests at prevailing short-term rates.

One question: Are you thinking about parking something like $100K–$200K for a few months, or a larger amount for a year or more? The amount and timeline would affect whether I'd recommend SGOV, direct T-bills, or a Treasury money-market fund.
